About the EU AI Act

The EU AI Act (Regulation 2024/1689) is the world's first comprehensive legal framework for artificial intelligence, adopted on 13 June 2024 and entering into force on 1 August 2024. It applies to providers, deployers, importers, and distributors of AI systems placed on the EU market or affecting EU residents — regardless of where the organisation is based.

The Act takes a risk-based approach: obligations scale with the potential harm an AI system can cause. Prohibited systems face an immediate ban; high-risk systems carry the heaviest compliance burden; limited-risk systems require basic transparency; minimal-risk systems are largely unregulated. General-purpose AI (GPAI) models, such as large language models, attract separate obligations tied to their systemic capabilities.

Key dates: Prohibited AI practices banned from Feb 2025; GPAI obligations apply from Aug 2025; high-risk system rules fully enforced from Aug 2026 (or Aug 2027 for certain regulated-sector systems).

Unacceptable risk

Prohibited AI — subliminal manipulation, social scoring, real-time biometric surveillance in public spaces (with exceptions).

High risk

Regulated sectors: biometrics, critical infrastructure, education, employment, essential services, law enforcement, migration, justice.

Limited risk

Transparency obligations — chatbots, deepfakes, and emotion-recognition systems must disclose AI involvement to users.

Minimal / no risk

No specific obligations. Voluntary codes of conduct encouraged. Applies to most AI systems (spam filters, AI games, etc.).
